## Essential distinctions for the paper

1. Environmental task admission is centralized; the four robot allocators maintain local knowledge/state and allocate through messages. The intervention changes when online tasks enter their knowledge, not a central assignment or a full reset.
2. AGX causal, AGX zero-compute, and RP2040 allocator-HIL are separate treatments. AGX peer messages have **40 ms mean delay with uniform ±10 ms jitter**; RP2040 missions use **fixed 40 ms**. Admission announcements have fixed 40 ms delay in both. Identical task manifests therefore do **not** make AGX/RP2040 a controlled processor-speed comparison.
3. Archived AGX exports contain misleading legacy `rp2040_*` aliases holding rounded host-proxy durations. They are not RP2040 observations. Use `agx_allocator_processor_work_s` for AGX measured work, and attested hardware `allocator_processor_work_s` for RP2040 work.
4. Every complete Eager/Count hardware block uses the same board and runtime lineage. V9/V10 must remain visible, but policy and lineage are not confounded within any of the 38 complete blocks. Four shared traces, not 82 missions or thousands of calls, supply independent scenario replication.

## Intervention, task knowledge, and distributed allocation

All policies admit the initial eight in **one initial epoch** at t=0, announced reliably at t=0.04 s; initial admission is exempt from ordinary batch-size rules. Ordinary batches use release-time/task-index FIFO order. Bounded is **B4/W10 seconds**, not W15/W30, and W limits environmental queue residence, not compute completion or task latency.

```text
at t=0: admit initial 8 together; reliably announce to every robot
on exogenous online release(s): append in (release_time, task_index) order
    Eager: while pending: admit first 1 and announce
    Count(B) or Bounded(B,W): while pending_count >= B:
        admit first B and announce
Bounded only: at oldest_pending_release + W:
    if a pending oldest task is still due: admit the current sub-B queue
after releases/completions and safe control actions:
    if no unreleased tasks, 0 < pending_count < B,
       and every previously admitted task is physically complete:
        admit pending as terminal_residual and announce
on robot receipt: add task knowledge; preserve goal/motion/claims/path;
    queue lightweight admission hook for its next safe allocator transaction
```

**VERIFIED:** The terminal residual predicate also applies to Bounded if it becomes eligible before its timeout. It is not a final-release flush and does not allow idleness, consensus, or task completion to piggyback unrelated pending work. With 42 online tasks, successful Count B2 has 21 ordinary online batches; B4 has ten full batches and a two-task residual; B8 has five full batches and a two-task residual. Eager has 42 one-task online admissions. Add the one initial epoch to those counts. A residual waits for all earlier admitted work, so Count does not bound its age. Primary outcomes must retain this rule; a residual-excluded analysis is diagnostic, not an alternative primary policy.

Robots initially know no tasks until receiving the environment's authentic admission topic. Future coordinates do not enter their allocator snapshots or resident native target registry. Authentication here is a reserved simulator sender/topic check, **not a cryptographic/radio authentication experiment**. Peer completions are learned from messages or local service, not by reading shared world completion truth. During a compute interval all received messages/admissions buffer in FIFO order; their original delivery timestamps remain recorded. During traversal, admission does not interrupt physical movement; the next allocator call runs at the safe boundary. A prior visit is explicitly reopened when its task is newly admitted. Sources: `S:known_visit_sim/core/reallocation.py:491–599,744–834`; `S:known_visit_sim/core/robot.py:198–288,397–479,889–898`.

This corrected lifecycle avoids the superseded movement-rebid feedback loop. `S:known_visit_sim/algorithms/CBAA.py:33–157` |

All candidate limits and commitment horizons are explicitly `None`; compatibility constants from older code do not impose Top-K or depth-3 planning. All targets have probability one, so shared probability-adjusted cost `distance + 8*(1−normalized_probability)` reduces to distance. Admission only invalidates active-set-dependent caches (`base.py:91–109`). Collision-triggered replanning can release local paths; completion and consensus repair are allocator-specific. The submission should name these as **the evaluated adapted implementations**, with retained execution state, message-driven online task incorporation, grid travel scoring, completion hooks, and recovery added/adapted for this experiment. Correctness/theoretical convergence results of original allocators do not transfer automatically.

Recovery is eligible only when local active tasks exist and no executable goal exists, at `max(no_goal_since,last_locally_observed_progress,last_recovery_attempt)+120 s`. The allocator repairs valid state and may expire at most one locally blocking peer claim, then runs ordinary selection. There is no global reset or world-truth oracle. Movement/received peer progress/knowledge affect the deadline. **Metric caveat:** `recovery_state_change_count` counts truthy recovery returns; a return can indicate an existing path or a claimable task, so it is not an independently verified number of distinct state mutations (`robot.py:714–723`; ACBBA recovery lines 131–174).

## Execution clock and timing boundaries

Four independent **virtual processors** share one mission clock. Each same-time call group freezes all robot pre-states before any authoritative call executes. Host calls physically execute sequentially; RP2040 calls for one mission also physically execute sequentially on that mission's single board. Each logical robot nevertheless becomes busy until its own `virtual_start + measured_duration`. Peers, releases, and the simulated bus continue in virtual time. The calling robot cannot move or start another control action during allocation; it completes an already-started traversal before beginning allocation. Outputs and goal selection become visible only at the modeled compute completion; queued arrivals can then request another call before motion.

At a timestamp, ordered queue events prioritize releases (0), timeout (1), movement and compute completion (2, stable insertion order within the priority), and control wake (3). Then the gate tests residual eligibility, the bus delivers due messages, and completed-mission detection precedes new allocation/control work. Same-time call groups and zero-duration microsteps drain without inserting a fictitious positive compute delay. The zero-duration guard allows further transactions only with new timed/input information; it prevents infinite zero-time polling. Mission stops at final required task completion, not final release. Sources: `S:known_visit_sim/core/scheduler.py:510–624,626–846,881–1041`.

The timed allocator transaction comprises FIFO decoded admission/peer-consensus/completion hooks, requested allocator-local recovery, and goal selection/repair/bidding. AGX uses elapsed `perf_counter_ns`; this is measured wall duration of the allocation boundary, not OS process-CPU accounting, so host descheduling can affect it. Injected host-proxy time rounds components to microseconds. Native MicroPython uses `ticks_us` with wrap-safe `ticks_diff`; its reported allocation timer wraps queued input application plus selection. Naturally triggered GC inside the timer remains included. Explicit pre-call GC is outside. Exclusions: USB/serial transport, message decoding, generic state/checkpoint restore, snapshots/hashing, outbound extraction and serialization, and journaling. Allocator-internal creation/queueing of entries while handling consensus is included; later outbound extraction is excluded. **A* routing/control and turning/travel are outside the allocation timer**, so this is not total onboard computing cost. Sources: `S:known_visit_sim/core/robot.py:698–790`; `H10:.../device/common/replay_worker.py:833–908`; native runtime `choose_goal`.

Zero-compute still executes and measures the authoritative allocator on the host, but schedules every modeled compute duration as exactly zero. Host measured work on zero-compute trajectories is an implementation diagnostic, not zero virtual processor work. Causal versus zero can produce different consensus/call/motion trajectories; their mission-time difference is not the sum of calls removed from a frozen schedule.

## Metrics to use (mission is the replicate)

| Paper quantity | Exact source/definition, unit, and inclusion |
|---|---|
| Completion | All 50 required tasks physically completed; distinguish completed, finite algorithmic noncompletion, and technical failure. An accepted technical execution can be algorithmically incomplete. |
| AGX allocator work | `agx_allocator_processor_work_s = Σ valid parity-passed authoritative transaction ns / 1e9`, processor-seconds (sum of elapsed call measurements). Includes all dispatched transactions, even ones whose virtual completion lies beyond final task completion; not clipped at mission end. |
| RP2040 allocator work | Hardware `allocator_processor_work_s = rp2040_allocator_processor_work_s = Σ attested valid transaction µs / 1e6`. Same full-dispatched-call sum convention. Historical AGX fields with RP2040 names are rounded host aliases, not physical timing. |
| Mean latency | `mean_release_to_completion_latency_s = mean(c_i−r_i)` over records actually completed. On successful missions n=50, combining initial n=8 with online n=42. On failures n<50 and the metric is only completed-task conditional; never present it as mission task responsiveness or assign uncompleted tasks zero latency. |
| Tail latency | `p95_release_to_completion_latency_s`: sorted completed latencies, nearest-rank value at 1-based `ceil(.95*n)`. On successful 50-task mission this is the 48th order statistic; for online-only n=42 it is the 40th. Do not silently substitute interpolated quantiles. |
| Online-only latency | Derive `c_i−r_i` only for `initially_visible=false` (42 tasks when complete). Necessary companion to interpret admission, since the eight initial tasks never experience online batching and timing can alter their execution. Keep the all-50 mission outcome primary. |
| Admission waiting | Task environmental queue residence `admission_time_s−released_time_s`; local delivery adds 0.04 s. `c_i−admission_time_s` includes delivery, buffered compute, allocation, and motion. Neither component alone identifies causal mechanism. |
| First current-goal latency | `first_current_goal_time_s−released_time_s` is first team execution-goal selection; `mean_admission_to_first_current_goal_latency_s` starts at environmental admission, **not robot-local receipt**. A local response metric must subtract the selected robot's actual receipt timestamp. |
| Mission duration | `mission_elapsed_time_s=max_i(c_i)−0`, seconds, only defined as a completed mission endpoint. For noncompletion export is null; `algorithmic_horizon_time_s` / simulated clock describe observed cutoff, not mission duration. |
| Calls / mean per-call | Count valid parity-passed transactions (including autonomous consensus/completion/recovery). Mean work per call = total work/calls. Calls are mechanisms, not independent sample-size inflation. Admission epochs are not equivalent to call count. |
| Communication | `logical_allocation_payload_bytes_sent_total` is canonical compact-JSON allocator payload bytes before transport compression/framing and **before fan-out**. It is not wire/radio bytes, radio airtime, or energy. One send can deliver to three peers; admission reaches all four. |
| Motion | `total_team_steps`: committed grid traversals summed across robots. `movement_time_s`: sum of completed traversal intervals; excludes turning, waiting, and uncommitted in-flight traversals when mission ends. Neither is elapsed mission time. |
| Host runtime | `host_program_runtime_s`: elapsed execution of the host runner including setup, emulation/replay, logging, transport and provider cleanup. It is neither mission duration nor summed allocator work. |

Do not use `sum_same_start_group_max_device_duration_s` as critical-path delay or add it to the clock. `allocator_parallel_critical_path_time_s` is deliberately zero in corrected outputs. Work/(4×mission time) is a normalization, not exact occupancy when full calls overhang the mission endpoint. First-claim/bundle incorporation/reassignment and bundle size have different meanings for one-task CBAA versus path/bundle methods; they are within-allocator mechanism diagnostics, not generic cross-allocator responsiveness scores. Core metric implementation: `S:known_visit_sim/core/reallocation.py:881–1178`.

## Stopping rule and interpretation

The producing event horizon is **251,000 processed robot/scheduler events** and stagnation threshold is **5,500 same-progress events** for 50 tasks. Progress signature includes task lifecycle, completed count, robot positions, and recovery return-count. A locally unexercised recovery opportunity postpones stagnation termination. The stopping test does not generically guarantee waiting for all future exogenous releases, in-flight computation, or queued movement. Therefore “algorithmic noncompletion” means noncompletion under this finite endpoint, not proof of permanent deadlock or inherent allocator unreliability. Preserve primary endpoints and use the companion diagnostic dossier for causal interpretation; increased-horizon results are sensitivity evidence. Sources: `S:known_visit_sim/core/scheduler.py:852–874,1018–1119`.

## Hardware and reproducibility limits

Each of four Pololu 3pi+ 2040 boards runs one worker/mission at a time, corresponding to AGX host cores 0–3. Each mission models **all four logical robots** on its board via one resident native runtime with complete checkpoints restored per call outside the timer. It is not four boards concurrently representing four robots of one physical mission. Motor-free allocator-HIL establishes device execution, state/output parity, and compute-duration evidence; simulated movement and communication do not validate real motor control, sensors, radio behavior, energy, or end-to-end real-time robot operation.

All successful summaries attest MicroPython 1.24 compiled `.mpy` builds with optimization level 0, one firmware hash, board UID, build/module-set hash and passed host/device parity. V9/V10 build identities and board UIDs are included in [campaign_provenance.json](campaign_provenance.json). V10 changed loading/heap/transfer behavior, not allocator policy, candidates, timer boundaries, or parity requirements. Its heap change can affect naturally triggered GC inside the timer, so preserved V9 timings remain a separate lineage. Detailed per-call attestations, native environment/deployment/preflight files, and raw state/transport logs are hash-indexed or referenced but absent from this compact checkout; summary-level parity is verified, full physical parity cannot be independently rerun here.
